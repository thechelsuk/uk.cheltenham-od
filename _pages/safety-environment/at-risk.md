## Emergencies That Could Affect Cheltenham

The Gloucestershire Local Resilience Forum, the partnership of emergency services, councils, the NHS and other organisations that plans for emergencies in the county, publishes a Community Risk Register. It names the emergencies most likely to affect Gloucestershire, or that would do the most harm, and scores each one for likelihood and impact.

The [{{ site.data["community-risk-register"].edition }} register](https://www.glosprepared.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/CRR-2024.pdf) lists seven top risks: terrorism, cyber attack, infectious disease, extreme weather, electricity supply failure, flooding and fuel supply disruption. Below, each risk sits on a matrix of likelihood against impact, with a short summary of the advice and links to the pages on this site where you can follow it live, from [flood warnings](/cheltenham-flood-warnings) and [power cuts](/cheltenham-power-cuts) to the [UK threat level](/cheltenham-security-alerts) and [cyber security](/cheltenham-cyber-security).

For phone numbers to call in an emergency, see our [helplines and contacts](/cheltenham-helpful-contacts-reports). More advice is on the forum's [Gloucestershire Prepared](https://www.glosprepared.co.uk/) site.

## Be Ready: What to Keep at Home

Most emergencies on the register, from power cuts and flooding to a loss of water or fuel, can leave a household without the usual services for a while. The government's [Be Ready](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/be-ready-for-emergencies/) guidance suggests keeping enough at home to manage for three days:

- **Water:** bottled water for three days, at 3 litres per person per day for drinking, plus more for cooking and washing.
- **Food:** non-perishable food for three days that doesn't need a fridge or cooking, such as tinned meat, fish or vegetables, dried fruit and nuts.
- **Light and warmth:** a torch with spare batteries (safer than candles), and blankets, sleeping bags and warm clothing.
- **News and power:** a battery or wind-up FM radio, with your local stations' frequencies written down, and a charged power bank.
- **Money and documents:** cash in notes and coins, cards, copies of ID and important documents, and important phone numbers written down.
- **Health:** a first aid kit and your essential medication. Request repeat prescriptions up to five working days before you need them.
- **Hygiene:** hand sanitiser, wet wipes and bathroom products.
- **Babies and pets:** ready-to-feed baby formula, nappies and wipes, and pet food and water.

Be Ready also has a [printable checklist](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/252/2026/10/Be-Ready-Checklist_English.pdf) and a [household emergency plan](https://beready.campaign.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/252/2026/10/Be-Ready_Household_Emergency_Plan_English_Final.pdf). If someone in your home needs extra help during a power or water cut, including households with children under 5, check whether you can join the [Priority Services Register](https://www.thepsr.co.uk/). To report a power cut, call 105.
