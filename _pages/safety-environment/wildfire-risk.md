## About Wildfire Risk Around Cheltenham

Wildfire risk around Cheltenham is usually low. Grass, gorse and woodland on the Cotswold escarpment can still dry out quickly in a hot spell, and Cleeve Hill sees more grass fires than anywhere else around the town. This page shows the Met Office Fire Severity Index for Cheltenham and the land within 10 km of the town centre: how severe a fire could become if one started today, and over the next five days.

The level runs from 1 (low) to 5 (exceptional). The page takes the highest level across the grid squares around town, so a high rating on the hills counts even when the town itself is lower. For the number of grassland, woodland and crop fires recorded around Cheltenham each year since 2010, see the [wildfire history page](/cheltenham-wildfire-risk/history).

### Preventing Wildfires

- Use barbecues only where they are allowed, never on dry grass, and never on open hillsides or in woodland. Make sure a barbecue is completely cold before you put it in a bin.
- Do not light campfires or bonfires on open land, and do not drop cigarettes, matches or glass, which can focus sunlight.
- Keep to paths on commons and hills when the level is high, and take all litter home.
- When the level is exceptional, check the [Natural England Open Access site](https://openaccess.naturalengland.org.uk/) before walking on open access land, as some areas may be closed.

### If You See a Fire

- Call 999 and ask for the fire service, even if you think someone else has already called. Give the location as precisely as you can, for example a car park, footpath or what3words address.
- Move away from the fire, upwind if you can, and do not try to put out anything larger than a small flame yourself.
- If smoke drifts over your home, keep windows and doors closed until it clears.

For more advice on outdoor fire safety, see [Gloucestershire Fire and Rescue Service](https://www.glosfire.gov.uk/).
