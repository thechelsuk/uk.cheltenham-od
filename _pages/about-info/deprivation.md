## Deprivation in Cheltenham

Cheltenham is one of the less deprived places in England. On the English Indices of Deprivation {{ dep.edition }}, it ranks **{{ dep.summary.rank_of_average_score }}th of {{ dep.summary.district_count }} local authority districts**, where 1 is the most deprived, and {{ dep.summary.least_deprived_20 }} of its {{ dep.summary.lsoa_count }} neighbourhoods are among the least deprived 20% in the country.

That average hides real differences within the town: {{ dep.summary.most_deprived_20 }} Cheltenham neighbourhoods are in the most deprived 20% in England, concentrated in the west and north of the town, a short distance from neighbourhoods that are among the least deprived anywhere. The map and tables below show where, and which kinds of deprivation, from income and employment to health, crime and housing, drive the difference.

The indices are the government's official measure of relative deprivation for small areas. They sit alongside our pages on the [local economy](/cheltenham-economy), [employment rates](/cheltenham-employment-history) and [earnings](/cheltenham-earnings-history), and our [child poverty](/cheltenham-child-poverty) page compares the share of children in low-income families in each ward. Each [ward page](/cheltenham-wards) also shows how its own neighbourhoods rank.
