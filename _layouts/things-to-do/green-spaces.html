Frequently Asked Questions

How Much Green Space Is There in Cheltenham?

  • Ordnance Survey maps {{ data.totals.sites }} publicly accessible or recreational green spaces in the borough, covering {{ data.totals.hectares | round }} hectares, or {{ data.totals.pct_of_borough }}% of its land. {{ parks.sites }} of them are public parks and gardens, together {{ parks.hectares | round }} hectares.

Which Ward Has the Most Green Space?

  • {{ greenest.name }}, where green space covers {{ greenest.pct_of_ward }}% of the ward ({{ greenest.hectares }} hectares){% if greenest.largest != "" %}, the largest named site being {{ greenest.largest }}{% endif %}. The table above compares all {{ data.wards.size }} wards.

Where Are the Allotments in Cheltenham?

  • There are {{ allotments.sites }} allotment and community growing sites, covering {{ allotments.hectares }} hectares. Filter the table by type to list them, or hide the other groups on the map. Cheltenham & District Allotment Holders' Association represents local plot holders and offers advice, and our clubs and societies page lists it among other groups to join.

Why Isn't the Countryside Around Cheltenham Included?

  • The map covers sites set aside for recreation, such as parks, sports grounds, allotments and churchyards, inside Cheltenham borough. Open countryside, commons, woodland and footpaths aren't part of this dataset, and nor are green spaces just over the borough boundary.

Why Are Some Sites Unnamed?

  • Ordnance Survey only names a site when it has a recognised name, so many playing fields, play spaces and churchyards are listed by their type and ward instead. Click the map pin to see exactly where one is.
