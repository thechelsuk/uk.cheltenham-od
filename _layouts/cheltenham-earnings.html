Frequently Asked Questions

What Does Median Pay Mean?

  • The median is the middle value: half of full-time employees earn more and half earn less. It is a better guide to typical pay than the average, which a small number of very high earners can pull upwards.

What Is the Difference Between Residents and Workplace Pay?

  • Residents pay counts full-time employees who live in Cheltenham, wherever they work. Workplace pay counts full-time employees whose job is in Cheltenham, wherever they live. Many people commute in and out of town, so the two figures differ.

Why Does the Figure Jump Around From Year to Year?

  • The figures come from a sample survey of employees, so for a town the size of Cheltenham the estimate can move by more than real pay does. The Reliability column shows the ONS's own rating of each year's estimate, from precise to unreliable. Look at the overall trend rather than any single year.

Why Does the Data Start in 2008?

  • The ONS earnings figures available for Cheltenham start in 2008, so this is as far back as the local data goes.

Are the Figures Adjusted for Inflation?

  • No. They are the cash amounts employees were paid in each year, before tax, so they do not show how far the money went.

Where Does This Data Come From?

  • The Office for National Statistics' Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings, accessed through Nomis. It is published once a year in the autumn, and this page is refreshed monthly so the new year appears soon after it is released. See employment rates for the labour market alongside pay.
