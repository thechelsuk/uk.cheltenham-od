Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Life Expectancy in Cheltenham?

  • Based on deaths in {{ life.year }}, a boy born in Cheltenham can expect to live to {{ life.male }} and a girl to {{ life.female }}, against {{ life.england_male }} and {{ life.england_female }} across England. These are averages for the whole town; the figures in the Life Expectancy table show how much they differ between its richest and poorest areas.

What Is Healthy Life Expectancy?

  • It is the number of years a person can expect to live in good health, based on how people rate their own health in the census. The gap between healthy life expectancy and life expectancy is roughly the number of years spent in poor health.

What Does "Better," "Similar" or "Worse" Mean?

  • It is the Office for Health Improvement and Disparities' own test of whether Cheltenham's figure is significantly different from England's, allowing for the margin of error in a town-sized sample. A small difference is often marked "Similar." Some measures have no better or worse direction and are not compared.

Why Are Some Figures Several Years Old?

  • Each measure comes from a different survey or register, with its own publishing timetable. Some, such as healthy life expectancy and wellbeing, combine several years of data to give a reliable figure for a town. The Period column shows the years each figure covers.

Where Can I Find a GP or Get Health Advice?

  • See GPs and pharmacies to find a practice near you. For urgent medical help that isn't an emergency, use NHS 111, and in an emergency call 999.
