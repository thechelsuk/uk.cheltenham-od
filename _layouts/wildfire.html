Frequently Asked Questions

What Is the Fire Severity Index?

  • The Met Office Fire Severity Index rates how severe a wildfire could become if one started, from 1 (low) to 5 (exceptional).
  • It is worked out from the weather: recent rainfall, drought, temperature, humidity and wind.
  • It does not forecast whether a fire will start. Most wildfires in England are started by people, by accident or on purpose.

What Happens at Exceptional (Level 5)?

  • At level 5, landowners and Natural England can close open access land to the public to reduce the chance of a fire starting.
  • Check the Natural England Open Access site for restrictions before walking on open hillsides such as Cleeve Common or Leckhampton Hill.

Which Area Does This Page Cover?

  • Every 10 km grid square that comes within {{ data.radius_km }} km of Cheltenham town centre: {% for sq in data.squares %}{{ sq.ref }}{% unless forloop.last %}, {% endunless %}{% endfor %}.
  • That takes in the Cotswold escarpment from Cleeve Hill to Crickley Hill, the Severn Vale towards Gloucester and Tewkesbury, and the land beyond, as smoke from a fire nearby can drift over the town.

How Often Is It Updated?

  • The Met Office issues a new forecast every morning for today and the next five days. This page picks it up several times a day.
  • Every day rated high (3) or above is recorded on the wildfire history page.

How Can I Get Alerts?

  • Subscribe to the wildfire risk feed in any RSS or feed reader. It lists every day forecast at high (3) or above.
  • When the level reaches very high (4) or exceptional (5) today or tomorrow, an alert also shows on the Cheltenham Open Data homepage.
