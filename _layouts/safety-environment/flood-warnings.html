Frequently Asked Questions

What Does the Typical Range Mean?

  • The Environment Agency publishes a typical range for each gauge, the band the river normally sits in. A level above it does not mean flooding, but it does mean the river is higher than usual, often after rain.

What Is the Highest on Record?

  • The highest level each gauge has ever recorded. Several of the gauges on the River Chelt and Hatherley Brook hold their record from the floods of June and July 2007, which are covered on our 2007 floods page.

Why Are Some Levels Close to Zero?

  • Levels are measured from each gauge's own zero point, not from sea level, and small streams can run very low in dry weather. Compare a reading with its own typical range rather than with other gauges.

How Often Is It Updated?

  • The gauges take a reading every 15 minutes. This page is refreshed every couple of hours, so for a fast-moving situation check the Environment Agency's live service.
