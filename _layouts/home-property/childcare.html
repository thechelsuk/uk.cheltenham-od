Frequently Asked Questions

What Do the Ofsted Outcomes Mean?

  • Outstanding, Good, Requires improvement and Inadequate are the overall grades from a full early years inspection.
  • Report card means the provider has been inspected under Ofsted's newer framework, which grades each area, such as inclusion and children's welfare, on a five-point scale from exceptional to urgent improvement instead of giving one overall grade. The full report has the grades.
  • Requirements met or not met applies to out-of-school clubs and providers on the Childcare Register only, which Ofsted checks against its requirements rather than grading.
  • Voluntary register providers, such as some tutors and holiday camps, have chosen to register with Ofsted; it only inspects them if it has concerns.
  • Not yet inspected usually means a provider registered recently.

Why Aren't Childminders Listed?

  • Most childminders work from their own homes, so Ofsted doesn't publish their names or addresses. Gloucestershire County Council's Glosfamilies directory lists childminders who have chosen to advertise.

Can I Get Funded Childcare Hours?

  • Many working parents of children from nine months old, and all three and four-year-olds, can get free childcare hours with a registered provider. Check what you're entitled to on the government's Childcare Choices site.

Does a Provider Have Places Available?

  • The number shown is how many children a provider is registered to care for at once, not how many places are free. Contact the provider directly to ask about availability.

Where Does This Data Come From?

  • Ofsted's register of childcare providers and their latest inspections, which it publishes every three months. The list is checked monthly. Each provider is placed at the centre of its postcode, so a pin is close to the setting rather than exactly on it.
