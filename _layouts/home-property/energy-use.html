Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Kilowatt Hour?

  • A kilowatt hour (kWh) is the unit on an energy bill: the energy used running something rated at 1 kilowatt for an hour. A gigawatt hour (GWh) is a million kWh.

What Is the Difference Between the Average and the Median?

  • The average (mean) adds up all the energy used and divides it by the number of meters. The median is the use of the home in the middle when every home is lined up from lowest to highest.
  • A few homes that use a lot, such as large houses or homes heated by electricity, pull the average up, so the median is usually lower and closer to what a typical home uses.

What Does Weather-Corrected Mean?

  • Homes use more gas in a cold year. The government adjusts gas figures to what would have been used in a year of typical weather, so years can be compared fairly. Electricity figures are not adjusted.

Why Do Some Areas Use More Energy Than Others?

  • Bigger homes, older homes that are harder to insulate and homes with more people tend to use more. Homes heated by electricity, such as many flats and homes with heat pumps, use more electricity and little or no gas.
  • Our Cheltenham home energy ratings page maps how energy efficient homes are across the same wards and small areas.

What Is a Small Area?

  • Small areas are the neighbourhoods the Office for National Statistics uses for its statistics (lower layer super output areas), each with around 1,500 people. Cheltenham has {{ data.lsoa.areas.size }}.
  • Ward figures add up the small areas within each ward. Where a small area crosses a ward boundary, it counts in the ward that holds most of it.

How Often Is This Page Updated?

  • The figures are published once a year, in December, for the year before. We check for new figures every month.
