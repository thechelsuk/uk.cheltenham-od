Frequently Asked Questions

What Is a Megawatt?

  • A megawatt (MW) is 1,000 kilowatts (kW), and measures how much power an installation can produce at full output. The average installation in Cheltenham is {{ borough.average_kw }} kW.
  • A megawatt hour (MWh) is the energy produced by running at 1 MW for an hour. The average Cheltenham home used {{ use.borough.electricity_mean_kwh_display }} kWh of electricity in {{ use.year }}, about {{ use.borough.electricity_mean_kwh | divided_by: 1000.0 | round: 1 }} MWh.

Why Is Almost All of Cheltenham's Renewable Energy Solar?

  • Rooftop solar panels fit on homes and businesses in a built-up town. Wind turbines, solar farms and biomass plants need open land, which a built-up town mostly doesn't have.

Does This Include Solar Panels on Homes?

  • Yes. The installation and capacity figures count every registered installation, including panels on house roofs. The project map only includes larger projects, of 150 kW or more, that have been through the planning system.

What Do the Project Statuses Mean?

  • Operational: built and generating or storing power.
  • Under construction: being built.
  • Approved: has planning permission but hasn't been built yet. Some approved projects are never built.
  • In planning: an application or appeal is waiting for a decision.
  • Refused, withdrawn or ended: refused, withdrawn, abandoned, or with permission that has run out, or a plant that has since closed.

Why Are Batteries Included?

  • Battery storage sites don't generate electricity, but they store it when there's plenty, such as on a sunny afternoon, and release it when it's needed, which lets the grid use more solar and wind power. The planning database records them alongside renewable projects.

How Can I Find Out About a Planning Application in Cheltenham?

  • Our Cheltenham planning applications page lists recent applications to Cheltenham Borough Council, with a map.

How Often Is This Page Updated?

  • We check for new figures every month. Installation and capacity figures are published once a year, in September, and the planning database every three months.
