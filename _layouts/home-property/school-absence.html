Frequently Asked Questions

What Do Persistent and Severe Absence Mean?

  • A school day has two sessions, morning and afternoon. A pupil is persistently absent if they miss 10% or more of their possible sessions, about one day a fortnight, and severely absent if they miss 50% or more.
  • The figures count all absence, authorised (such as illness or medical appointments) and unauthorised (such as an unapproved holiday or an unexplained absence). They cover pupils aged 5 to 15 at state-funded primary, secondary and special schools.
  • The Department for Education counts pupil enrolments rather than pupils, so a child who moved school during the year is counted at each school. Across England, enrolments are about 4% higher than the number of pupils.

Why Is There No Figure for 2019/20?

  • The Department for Education cancelled its 2019/20 absence releases during the COVID-19 pandemic.
  • From 2020/21 until April 2022, schools recorded sessions that pupils couldn't attend because of COVID-19 rules under a separate code that isn't counted as absence, so take care comparing those years with others.

Which Figures Should I Compare?

  • The full school year figures, published each March, are the definitive ones. The autumn and spring term figures, published each October, come out sooner but cover only part of the year, when winter illness is at its height, so the Department for Education treats them as an early indication.
  • A school's absence depends on its pupils as well as the school, for example on how many have long-term health conditions or special educational needs, so the figures are a starting point for questions rather than a ranking. Small schools' percentages can move a lot from one year to the next because of a handful of pupils.
