The site looks consistent because a small set of components is reused everywhere. Before adding a class or a one-off size, check this page. If something new is needed, it becomes a component here first: say what it is, which pages it changes, and how it fits the rules below, then agree it before building.

## Units

Pick the unit by what a size is measured against:

- **`em`** for anything sized against the text it sits in: icons in text, labels, pill padding.
- **`rem`** for layout: spacing between blocks and column widths.
- **`%`** for something sized to its own container, such as an icon inside a circle.
- **`px`** only for borders, hairlines and screen-width breakpoints.
- **`ch`** only for the line length of body text (the `72ch` measure). A `ch` is the width of "0", so it under-measures capitals and letter spacing.

Derive a value from what it has to fit, measured at the element's own font size, and say so in a comment.

## Colours

- Colours come from tokens in `assets/style.css` and `assets/themes.css`, never from a hex value in a layout.
- Each section of the site has a theme (`theme-<name>` in `themes.css`) that sets `--accent`, `--accent-dark` and `--accent-text`. Headings, list markers and pills follow the accent.
- Every token has a dark-mode value under `[data-theme="dark"]`.
- Never signal anything by colour alone: pair it with an icon, a shape or text.

## Headings

- AP title case: capitalise the first and last word and every word of four or more letters ("From", "With", "Near"); keep articles, conjunctions and prepositions of three letters or fewer lower case ("a", "and", "of", "off", "on", "to"). "over" is the site's one exception and stays lower case.
- `_python/local/x-heading-auditor.py _site` checks every heading against this rule, using `helper.title_case()`, which the jobs fetcher also uses for GOV.UK job titles.
- Phrasal verbs keep their particle capitalised ("Back Up", "Add Up", "Turn On"), as AP does.
- Other people's text keeps its own case: headlines quoted from other publishers (a heading that is wholly a link to their article), and registered names such as schools, care homes and football referees. The auditor skips them.
- One `h1` per page, from the layout. Sections are `h2`, which take the accent underline; FAQ questions are `h3`.

## Page Header

- Breadcrumbs, the `h1`, then the page description.
- `header-facts`: the lighter boxes for key dates and facts, and sub-page links. Related pages go in body text, not here.
- `header-links`: under the facts, the page's one or two official outside links (the council's checker, the club's site, a feed), each ending with an arrow.
- The "In this section" row (`_includes/section-links.html`) sits under the header on pages with sub-pages. Keep it as it is.

## Pills and Arrows

Two pill styles, each with one job. Don't add others.

- **Call to action:** `<p class="action-row"><a class="pill-link" href="...">Text &rarr;</a></p>`, always ending with an arrow. This covers "See all…" links, apply and app store links on adverts, directions, pagination and any link standing on its own line. In Markdown: `[Text &rarr;](url){: .pill-link}` with `{: .action-row}` on the next line.
- **In this section:** generated by `section-links.html`, with the current page marked.

Where arrows go:

- A link inside a sentence is a plain link, with no arrow.
- A short link in a table cell (such as "Record →") may keep its arrow.
- Sponsor cards and the referral offer card are calls to action too, but they are cards by design, not pills.
- `header-links` keep their arrows (see Page Header).

## Inline Styles

Don't use `style=""` attributes; put the styling in a class in `assets/style.css`. An inline style is a sign something should be a component. Two exceptions:

- **A data value** that can only come from the data, such as a bar's width or a source's colour, is passed as a CSS custom property and nothing else, for example `style="--bar-width: 40%"` or `style="--swatch: #3182bd"`. The class does all the styling with `var(--bar-width)`.
- **Embed code for other sites**, such as the widgets, which can't rely on the site's style sheet.

Right-aligned Markdown table columns (`--:`) are fine: `_plugins/table_alignment.rb` swaps Kramdown's inline style for the `number` class.

The same goes for `<style>` blocks: styles live in `assets/style.css`, not in a layout or include. The one exception is `_includes/newsletter-signup.html`, which keeps its own block until the newsletter sign-up is replaced, so it can be tidied up then.

## Tables

Follow [Displaying Data](/admin/guide/displaying-data): `table-scroll` around a `data-table`, `data-sortable` where sorting helps, `number`, `postcode` and `date` classes on columns, one value per cell, and dates as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`. Markdown tables in posts and pages get the same treatment automatically from `_plugins/table_alignment.rb`: the `table-scroll` wrapper and `data-table` class, `number` for right-aligned (`--:`) columns, and `postcode` and `date` for cells that are wholly a postcode or a date. A table doesn't need a key, but when it has one, it is the key style below.

## Key and Attribution

Two small-print lines, each a list item in small text with the accent marker.

**Key** (circle marker): explains a table's or chart's short headings and symbols. It starts "Key:" automatically.

{% include key.html text="**Pld** played, **W** won, **D** drawn, **L** lost, **F** goals for, **A** goals against" %}

```liquid
{% raw %}{% include key.html text="**Pld** played, **W** won, **D** drawn, **L** lost" %}{% endraw %}
```

**Attribution** (square marker): where the data comes from, its licence and when it was last updated.

{% include attribution.html text="Data from [Police.uk](https://data.police.uk/), published under the Open Government Licence v3.0. Last updated 2026-11-03." %}

```liquid
{% raw %}{% include attribution.html text="Data from [SOURCE](URL), published under the LICENCE. Last updated YYYY-MM-DD." %}{% endraw %}
```

- Use both in posts. The text is Markdown, so bold and links work.
- Data pages keep `<p class="attribution"><small>` under "About This Data", with the freshness include. It shows as the same square-marked line.
- The football pages write the key's HTML directly, which gives the same result as `key.html`.

## Kind Markers

A coloured circle with an icon, and beside it the kind's label in capitals as a kicker, on its own line, with the title (or, in a post's byline, the author and dates) underneath. Used for news, alerts and classifieds on the home page and `/news`, the weather warnings, and the byline on every post. The kicker needs no fixed width, so every list lines up the same way whatever its labels. See every kind on the [kinds page](/admin/kinds).

{% include admin/kind-examples.html %}

- **One colour per group:** news uses the theme accent, alerts amber (`--kind-alerts`), classifieds slate (`--kind-classifieds`). The icon and label say which kind.
- **Kinds live in `_data/kinds.yml`:** label, icon path and a demo title. A new kind is one entry there; a new group needs a colour token and a `.kind-<group>` class.
- **News posts:** set `kind:` in front matter, or leave it out to get analysis (with a `finding:`) or update.
- **Alerts:** a fetcher passes its kind key to `helper.alert_rows()`.
- **Classifieds:** the kind is the ad's `category`, which matches `classified_categories` in `_config.yml`.
- **Icons** are Material Icons, except the Update megaphone (from the site logo) and the Flood cloud (Material Symbols).
- To check a new kind, see it on the [kinds page](/admin/kinds), which shows every kind with its demo title.

## News Posts

The headline is the page's `h1`, in the header. Below the header:

- **Byline:** the kind marker's circle, then the kicker line with the author ("ROUNDUP · By Mat Benfield"), and the date underneath ("Published 3 November 2026", plus "Updated" when there is one), the same layout as the news lists. A thin rule under the byline marks where the article starts.
- **Lede:** the post's first paragraph, set a size larger straight after the byline. Two or three sentences that say what happened, why it matters to someone in Cheltenham, and the standout story, not a list of counts. On analysis posts the layout lifts the first paragraph above the quoted finding and chart, so every post opens the same way; the feed keeps the post in its written order.
- **Credit:** posts are written for the site and edited by Mat, who is named as the author.

## Newsletter

The Cheltenham Week Ahead covers the coming week, and its What's On section looks up to a month ahead, because people may need to book. What's On is for things to go to: parties, gigs, concerts, markets, and specific closures or openings. Consultations, council decisions and planning are not newsletter items; if one matters, it's a news post.

Most events come from the feeds in `_data/event-sources.yml`. When you find one they miss, from a source you're reading, a venue's own listings or a reader, add it to `_data/candidate-events.json`:

- `title`, `date` (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and, for anything longer than a day, `end_date`
- `time` (`HH:MM`) and `venue`, if the source gives them
- `link`: the event's page on the organiser's or venue's own site
- `note`: only a detail the source itself states, such as "Free entry". Leave it out rather than write one

Every entry needs a date and a link, or the newsletter skips it. `weekly-newsletter.py` removes events once they have ended, each time it runs, so the file never needs tidying by hand. The entries appear in the same review as the feed events, so add events, not reminders to check things.

## Dates

- In tables and map popups: `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`, or `YYYY-MM-DD`.
- Everywhere else, written out: "23 February 2027". That includes the "Published" and "Updated" dates in a post's byline.

## Empty States

A "nothing to show" message is a `blockquote`, not a plain paragraph.

> There are no power cuts affecting Cheltenham at the moment.
