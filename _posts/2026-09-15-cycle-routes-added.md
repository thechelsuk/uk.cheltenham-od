Cheltenham Open Data has published a new [cycle routes page](/cheltenham-cycle-routes), mapping dedicated cycle tracks and on-road cycle lanes in and around Cheltenham.

Most of what's mapped is ordinary street-level provision — a track alongside a road, or a marked lane on it — sourced from OpenStreetMap, the same map data that powers most cycle-routing apps. A couple of routes are notable enough to call out on their own: the Honeybourne Line, a traffic-free path along the old railway towards Bishop's Cleeve, and National Cycle Network Route 41, which passes through town between Gloucester and Bishop's Cleeve.

This joins [car parks](/cheltenham-car-parks), [EV charging](/cheltenham-ev-charging) and [bus data](/cheltenham-bus-data) under Getting Around.

## FAQs

### What Does This Page Show?

- A map of dedicated cycle tracks and on-road cycle lanes near Cheltenham, plus a table of named/numbered routes (the Honeybourne Line and NCN 41) with how much of each falls within the mapped area.

### Where Does the Data Come From?

- [OpenStreetMap](https://www.openstreetmap.org/), queried via the Overpass API and released under the Open Database Licence (ODbL).

### How Often Is It Updated?

- Monthly — cycling infrastructure doesn't change often, so it's refreshed on the same schedule as car parks and points of interest.

### My Local Route Isn't Shown — Why?

- Coverage depends entirely on what's been mapped in OpenStreetMap. If a track or lane is missing or wrong, it can be fixed at [openstreetmap.org](https://www.openstreetmap.org/) and will appear here next update.
