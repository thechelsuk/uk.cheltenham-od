Cheltenham Open Data has updated its [Fix My Street page](/cheltenham-fix-my-street), turning a plain list of report links into a map and a sortable table of the latest street and highways problems reported around Cheltenham.

Reports are grouped into plain-English categories such as potholes, trees and vegetation, footpaths and fly-tipping, so you can see at a glance what is being reported. There is also a new [report history page](/cheltenham-fix-my-street/history) with every report since 21 June 2026 as a JSON file to download, plus monthly counts, so problems don't vanish from view after a day or two.

## FAQs

### What Does the Fix My Street Page Show?

- The latest reports from [FixMyStreet](https://www.fixmystreet.com) within 10km of Cheltenham town centre, each with its title, category, date reported, a short description and a link to the original report. They are plotted on a map and listed in a table you can sort by any column.

### What Is New?

- A map of where reports are, a Google Maps pin on every row, cleaner report titles (council-generated titles like "TMC - Defects - Vegetation problem" now read "Highways defect: Vegetation"), a short description for each report, and a downloadable history file.

### Where Does the Data Come From?

- FixMyStreet, the independent reporting service run by [mySociety](https://www.mysociety.org), through its public RSS feed for the Cheltenham area. Report text is written by members of the public.

### How Often Is It Updated?

- Hourly. The live feed only shows the newest handful of reports, so the history page is topped up from each hourly check.

### How Far Back Does the History Go?

- Every report since 21 June 2026 within 10km of the town centre, growing as new reports arrive, as a JSON file you can download from the history page. It covers reports sent to Gloucestershire County Council and Cheltenham Borough Council, so a few reports for neighbouring councils may be missing. Older reports remain viewable on FixMyStreet itself.

### Why Are Some Reports Outside Cheltenham?

- The feed covers a 10km radius around the town centre, which takes in places like Bishop's Cleeve and Churchdown, and parts of the neighbouring Tewkesbury and Cotswold districts. The map shows where each report actually is.

### Who Fixes the Problems Reported?

- It depends on the problem. Gloucestershire County Council looks after roads, pavements, street lights, road signs, drains and public footpaths, while Cheltenham Borough Council handles litter, fly-tipping, graffiti and parks. FixMyStreet sends each report to the right authority for you.

### Why Are There No Photos?

- Reports are written and photographed by members of the public, so the page shows only a short text description. Click through to the original report on FixMyStreet to see any photos, the location and the council's response.

### How Do I Report a Problem?

- Visit [FixMyStreet](https://www.fixmystreet.com), enter a postcode or street name, drop a pin on the map at the exact spot, and add a short description and a photo if you can.
