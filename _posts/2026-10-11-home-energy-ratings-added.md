Cheltenham Open Data has published a new [home energy ratings page](/cheltenham-energy-ratings), showing how energy efficient the town's homes are.

Using the latest Energy Performance Certificate for more than 43,000 homes, the page shows how many are rated each letter from A to G, and maps every ward and neighbourhood by the share of homes rated C or better. You can also shade the map by any single rating, such as where the most F and G rated homes are. Each [ward page](/cheltenham-wards) now has its own energy ratings section too.

Just under half of Cheltenham's homes with a certificate are rated C or better, the level the government wants as many homes as possible to reach by 2035. The typical home scores 68 on the energy scale behind the letters, one point short of a C, but more than nine in ten could reach C or better with the improvements their own certificates recommend.

The page also breaks ratings down by type of home, tenure and main heating. Homes with a heat pump score highest, and flats do better than houses.

## FAQs

### What Does the Energy Ratings Page Show?

- The number and share of homes in each rating from A to G, the share rated C or better (C+), the median rating and energy score, how many homes could reach C, a map of every ward and small neighbourhood area, and tables by ward, area, type of home, tenure and heating.

### Where Does the Data Come From?

- The Energy Performance of Buildings Register, published by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government under the Open Government Licence. Only area totals are shown, never individual homes.

### Can I Look Up My Own Home?

- Yes, on GOV.UK's [find an energy certificate](https://www.gov.uk/find-energy-certificate) service, which lists the certificate for any home that has one.

### Why Does C Matter?

- Band C is the government's benchmark for an energy-efficient home, and privately rented homes will need to reach it by October 2030.
