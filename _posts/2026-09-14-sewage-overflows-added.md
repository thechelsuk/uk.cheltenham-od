Our [flood warnings page](/cheltenham-flood-warnings) is now "Cheltenham Water & Flooding" — alongside the existing Environment Agency flood alerts, it shows near-real-time sewage overflow status for the River Chelt and the brooks that feed it.

Severn Trent Water fits an Event Duration Monitor (EDM) to each storm overflow, recording exactly when it starts and stops discharging untreated wastewater during heavy rainfall. We pull that feed via Water UK's National Storm Overflow Hub, filtered to the 12 monitored overflows within 4 miles of Cheltenham, and show them on a map and sortable table alongside the flood alerts.

## FAQs

### What Does This Data Show?

- Whether each storm overflow near Cheltenham is currently discharging, when it last did, and for how long — refreshed several times a day.

### Where Does the Data Come From?

- Severn Trent Water's Event Duration Monitors, published via Water UK's [National Storm Overflow Hub](https://www.stwater.co.uk/in-my-area/storm-overflow-map/). No formal open licence is stated, but it's published as a free public data feed.

### Does This Tell Me About Water Quality?

- Not directly. It tracks discharge *activity*, not chemical water quality like nitrates or bacteria levels — a storm overflow discharging is a normal, permitted release valve for the sewer network during heavy rain, not necessarily a sign of a problem. See the page for more on what EDM data does and doesn't cover.

### How Often Is It Updated?

- Several times a day, alongside the existing flood alert refresh. Severn Trent aims to publish each discharge event within about an hour of it happening.
