Cheltenham Open Data has published a new [broadband speeds page](/cheltenham-broadband-internet-speeds).

Pulling Ofcom Connected Nations data, the page shows a map of every ward across GL50–GL54, shaded by how much of each ward can get gigabit broadband — plus tables breaking coverage down by ward and by speed tier.

## FAQs

### What Does the Broadband Page Show?

- A map shading each Cheltenham and Gloucestershire ward by its gigabit availability, a table of every ward with superfast, ultrafast and gigabit figures, and a summary of how many postcodes fall into each speed tier.

### Where Does the Broadband Data Come From?

- Ofcom's [Connected Nations](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/phones-and-broadband/coverage-and-speeds/) programme, which publishes fixed broadband coverage for every UK postcode. Ward boundaries come from the [ONS Open Geography Portal](https://geoportal.statistics.gov.uk/).

### How Often Is It Updated?

- Ofcom refreshes Connected Nations roughly twice a year, so the page updates whenever a new release lands. The "last updated" date is at the top of the page.

### What Do the Speed Tiers Mean?

- Superfast (30 Mbps+), ultrafast entry (100 Mbps+), ultrafast high (300 Mbps+) and gigabit-capable (1000 Mbps+). A ward is banded by the best tier available to at least half its premises — there's a full table explaining each one.

### Is This the Speed I'll Actually Get?

- No — it shows what's available to order, not a guarantee. Your real speed depends on your provider, package and the line to your door, so a fast-looking ward can still hide a slower street.

### Why Wards and Not Postcodes?

- There are several thousand postcodes across GL50–GL54 — far too many to read on a map. Grouping them into wards keeps it legible, while the table holds the ward-level detail.
