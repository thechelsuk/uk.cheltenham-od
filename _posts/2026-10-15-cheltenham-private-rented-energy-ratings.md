Cheltenham Open Data analysed the latest Energy Performance Certificate for every home in Cheltenham that has one. Of the 8,746 whose certificate records them as privately rented, 4,571 (52.3%) are rated D, E, F or G. From 1 October 2030, privately rented homes in England and Wales will need to reach the equivalent of band C. Our [home energy ratings page](/cheltenham-energy-ratings) maps every ward and neighbourhood.

## Key Figures

- **4,571** privately rented homes are rated below C: 3,452 at D, 1,037 at E and 82 at F or G.
- **4,170** of them (91.2%) could reach C or better with the improvements recommended on their own certificates.
- **2,598** (57%) are in five central wards: Lansdown, All Saints, Pittville, College and Park.
- **47.7%** of privately rented homes are rated C or better, against 40.9% of owner-occupied homes.
- **8,746** homes are recorded as privately rented, against 12,378 privately rented households in the 2021 Census, so the true numbers are higher.

## Where the Homes Are

The homes below C are concentrated in the centre of town. Lansdown has the most, 735, followed by All Saints with 575, Pittville with 444, College with 427 and Park with 417. In each of these wards, between 57% and 64% of the privately rented homes on the register are below C.

Elsewhere the numbers are much smaller. Outside the centre, only St Paul's (375) and St Peter's (218) have more than 150, and most wards have between 90 and 135.

## Better Than Owner-Occupied Homes

Privately rented homes are not the least efficient in Cheltenham. 47.7% are rated C or better, against 40.9% of owner-occupied homes, and their median energy score of 68 is one point higher. Social rented homes do best, at 73.5% rated C or better.

## What the 2030 Standard Means

Since April 2020, privately rented homes have needed a rating of at least E unless they have an exemption. The government has confirmed that from 1 October 2030 they will need to reach the equivalent of C, with a cap of £10,000 on what a landlord must spend on each home. The rules still need Parliament's approval, and compliance will be measured with new energy performance metrics.

On today's certificates, 4,170 of Cheltenham's 4,571 privately rented homes below C could reach C or better with the improvements their certificates list, such as insulation, double glazing or a more efficient boiler.

## Notes to Editors

- **Method:** each home counts once, using its latest Energy Performance Certificate. Homes are placed in wards by their postcode. "Could reach C" uses each certificate's potential rating, the rating the home would have if its recommended improvements were made.
- **Sources:** the Energy Performance of Buildings Register (Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government); Census 2021 table TS054, tenure, via Nomis; and the government's response to its consultation [improving the energy performance of privately rented homes](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/improving-the-energy-performance-of-privately-rented-homes-2025-update).
- **Caveats:** tenure is recorded when a certificate is issued, so a home let using the certificate from its last sale counts as owner-occupied, and 8,875 certificates in Cheltenham don't record tenure. The figures are therefore minimum counts. Ratings are under the current methodology; the 2030 standard will be measured with new metrics, which may place some homes differently.
- **Data and updates:** the [home energy ratings page](/cheltenham-energy-ratings) is updated monthly. For questions or interviews, use the [contact page](/contact).

## FAQs

### Which Cheltenham Wards Have the Most Rented Homes Below C?

- Lansdown (735), All Saints (575), Pittville (444), College (427) and Park (417), all in the centre of town.

### What Do Landlords Need to Do by 2030?

- Privately rented homes in England and Wales will need to reach the equivalent of EPC band C by 1 October 2030, or have a valid exemption, with spending capped at £10,000 a home.

### How Can I Check a Rented Home's Rating?

- Search by postcode on GOV.UK's [find an energy certificate](https://www.gov.uk/find-energy-certificate) service. Landlords must give tenants a copy of the certificate.
