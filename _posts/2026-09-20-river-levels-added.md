Cheltenham Open Data has added live river levels and rainfall to the [flood warnings page](/cheltenham-flood-warnings), and a history of the summer 2007 floods to the [flood history page](/cheltenham-flood-warnings/history).

The flood warnings page now has a map, a table and charts for the Environment Agency's river level gauges on the River Chelt, Hatherley Brook, Lilley Brook and Wymans Brook, and its rainfall gauges nearby. Each gauge shows its latest level, whether that is within its typical range, and the highest level it has ever recorded. Charts show the last 48 hours of readings, and a further chart keeps the highest level each day, which builds up a record beyond the four weeks the Environment Agency keeps.

The flood history page tells the story of the June and July 2007 floods, when around 600 properties flooded in Cheltenham. It also lists the highest level each gauge has recorded, and several of those records date from June or July 2007.

## FAQs

### What Do the New Sections Show?

- Current river levels and rainfall around Cheltenham with a status for each gauge, 48-hour charts, a daily peak chart, and the history of the 2007 floods.

### Where Does the Data Come From?

- The Environment Agency's [flood monitoring API](https://environment.data.gov.uk/flood-monitoring/doc/reference), published under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The 2007 history draws on Cheltenham Borough Council's and Gloucestershire County Council's flood reports, which are linked on the page.

### How Often Is It Updated?

- The gauges read every 15 minutes and the page is refreshed every couple of hours.

### Is This a Flood Warning Service?

- No. For warnings and the latest situation, use the Environment Agency's [live service](https://check-for-flooding.service.gov.uk/). This page shows readings alongside the warnings the Environment Agency has issued for the area.
